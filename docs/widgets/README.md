# Widgets

A widget shows live Home Assistant data inside a dashboard. You pick *what* to show
(its **sources**), set *how* it looks (its **options**) and resize its frame; the
widget lays itself out to fit.

This page is generated by `python -m scripts.widget_docs`, so it always says what the
widgets do. To write your own widget see [the widget SDK](../widget-sdk.md).

## Options every widget has

| Option | Key | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Show frame | `showFrame` | Draws the frame and the background. Turn it off to place the content straight onto the dashboard. |
| Corner radius | `cornerRadius` | How round the corners of the frame are. |
| Color | `color` | The color of everything drawn: text, icons, lines and the frame. |
| Background | `background` | What is filled behind it. |
| Font | `font` | Any font that comes with OpenDisplay Studio or that you installed in Settings. |

Colors are picked from the colors of the display, so white on black is two colors, and on
a display with red and yellow you can have yellow on red. A few widgets also have a
**Text size**: leave it on Auto to fit the frame, or type the size you want.

## Battery

*The charge of a battery as a battery that fills up, with its percentage.*

| Default size | Smallest | Id |
|---|---|---|
| 200 × 100 px | 24 × 24 px | `battery` |

<p align="center"><img src="images/battery-1.png" alt="A charged battery in the colors of a Spectra 6 display" width="200"><br><sub>A charged battery in the colors of a Spectra 6 display</sub></p>

Shows one battery entity as a battery that fills up in proportion to its charge, with
the percentage beside it (or under it in a tall frame). Pick a battery sensor (a
percentage) or a binary sensor that says whether its battery is low; the second is drawn
nearly empty and marked "Low", or full and marked "OK".

At or below **Low battery at or below** the battery and its text are drawn in the
highlight color of the display. The battery lies down in a wide frame and stands up in a
tall one, or as you choose with **Orientation**. The size of the text comes from the
frame and not from the number, so batteries of one size are laid out alike.

With **Percentage: inside the battery** the number is drawn in the middle of the
battery, which takes less room. It is outlined in the color of the background, whatever
the charge, so it reads on the fill and off it and looks the same at every level.

Turn off the name and the percentage (or put the percentage inside) and the battery fills the
whole widget: make the widget small and put it in a corner of the display to show the
charge of the e-paper display itself. **Style: segments** draws the charge as up to ten
rounded segments of one size, fewer when the battery is short; the segments that are full
follow the charge, rounded to the nearest, and at least one while there is any charge.

### Sources

| Source | What to pick | Per pick |
|---|---|---|
| Entity | one sensor or binary_sensor entity | Label |

### Options

**Content**

| Option | Key | Values | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Show name | `showName` | on / off | on |
| Show percentage | `showPercentage` | on / off | on |
| Style | `style` | one of `solid`, `segments` | `solid` |
| Percentage | `percentagePlacement` | one of `beside`, `inside` | `beside` |
| Orientation | `orientation` | one of `auto`, `horizontal`, `vertical` | `auto` |

**Thresholds**

| Option | Key | Values | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low battery at or below | `lowLevel` | number 0 to 100 | `20` |
| Low battery color | `alertColor` | color | `accent` |

## Calendar

*Events of one or more calendars as upcoming days, an agenda or a grid of weeks.*

| Default size | Smallest | Id |
|---|---|---|
| 800 × 480 px | 240 × 140 px | `calendar` |

<p align="center"><img src="images/calendar-1.png" alt="Upcoming events in the colors of a Spectra 6 display" width="380"><br><sub>Upcoming events in the colors of a Spectra 6 display</sub></p>

One widget with three views of the same calendars, chosen with **View**. Pick one or
more calendars; give each a label, a color and an icon of its own. Events of several
calendars are merged, identical ones are shown once, and the color of the calendar
marks its events.

* **Upcoming events** lists the next days (3 by default) in as many columns as fit the
  frame, each day under a heading that is filled for today. Events are numbered, all-day
  events carry a hash, and times, descriptions and places can be turned on or off.
  A bar at the bottom names the view and the calendars. What does not fit is counted
  as `+N more`.
* **Agenda** gives every day with events a bar and every event a line: when it starts,
  its title and description, when it ends. It shows as many days as the frame holds.
* **Weeks** is a grid of up to six weeks, starting from this week or from the first of
  the month, on the weekday you choose. All-day and multi-day events are bars over
  their days (across rows too), events of a day are listed in its cell, weekends are
  dotted and today is marked. Week numbers and the month can be added.

Events that have ended are hidden in the two lists (turn that off with *Hide events
that have ended*), and events can be left out by a word they contain or by their exact
name. An event without a title is shown as "Busy".

Home Assistant gives a calendar no guests and no color per event, so there is no filter
for declined invitations, and the color is that of the calendar.

| Calendars | one or more calendar entity | Label, Color, Icon |

## Gauge

*The value of a sensor on a half dial, with a marker or a filled arc.*

| Default size | Smallest | Id |
|---|---|---|
| 240 × 160 px | 96 × 64 px | `gauge` |

<p align="center"><img src="images/gauge-1.png" alt="Ranges: green, yellow from 40 and red from 80, as for the load of a processor" width="240"><br><sub>Ranges: green, yellow from 40 and red from 80, as for the load of a processor</sub></p>

Shows the value of one numeric sensor on a half dial. A marker across the arc points
at the value (or, with **Style: arc**, the arc is filled up to it), and the value is
written in the middle, with its unit.

**Scale** sets the lowest and the highest value the dial shows. A value beyond either end
is drawn at that end.

**Ranges** color the arc, as many as you want. Add one for each color and say where it
starts; it lasts until the next one starts. The first one has no start to give: it begins
at the lowest value. Green, then yellow from 40 and red from 80 is the usual load of a
processor; on a scale from 0 to 1 the same ranges start at 0.4 and 0.8. Leave out the
first range and the arc has no color before the second one starts. The colors are those
of your display. A filled arc takes the color of each range it passes.

The ticks are one for every tenth of the scale, and under the ends of the arc are the
lowest and the highest value. In a small frame the ticks and then the limits and the name
are left out, so the dial itself is as big as it can be.

### Sources

| Source | What to pick | Per pick |
|---|---|---|
| Entity | one sensor or input_number or number entity | Label |

### Options

**Content**

| Option | Key | Values | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Show name | `showName` | on / off | on |
| Show value | `showValue` | on / off | on |
| Show unit | `showUnit` | on / off | on |
| Decimals | `decimals` | one of `auto`, `0`, `1`, `2`, `3` | `1` |
| Style | `style` | one of `needle`, `arc` | `needle` |
| Show ticks | `showTicks` | on / off | on |
| Show minimum and maximum | `showLimits` | on / off | on |

**Scale**

| Option | Key | Values | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum | `min` | number | `0` |
| Maximum | `max` | number | `100` |

**Ranges**

| Option | Key | Values | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ranges | `ranges` | list of colored ranges | `[]` |

## Thermometer

*A temperature on a classic round dial with a needle.*

| Default size | Smallest | Id |
|---|---|---|
| 200 × 200 px | 96 × 96 px | `thermometer` |

<p align="center"><img src="images/thermometer-1.png" alt="A processor, green, yellow from 60 and red from 85" width="200"><br><sub>A processor, green, yellow from 60 and red from 85</sub></p>

Shows the value of one temperature sensor on a classic round dial, the kind that hangs
on a wall: a scale of ticks and numbers over three quarters of a circle, a needle, and
the temperature written under its hub with the name of the sensor.

**Scale** sets the lowest and the highest temperature and how far apart the numbers
are; there is a short tick between them. A temperature beyond either end is drawn at that
end. The needle takes the color you pick, the accent color of the display by default.

**Ranges** put a band of color along the edge of the dial: add one for each color and say
where it starts, and it lasts until the next one starts. Blue while cold, green at working
heat and red when too hot is a boiler; the colors are those of your display.

Several of them make a dashboard of temperatures: the outdoor and indoor temperature, the
flow and the return of a boiler, the hot water tank, the processor of a computer. Give each
its own scale. In a small frame the numbers of the scale give way first, then the name, so
the dial stays as big as it can be.
